# P39u record: phone uploads that never landed, and a failed one that said nothing

Branch `p39-upload-fix`, from master fbb27a5e (alpha 0.58.1). No version bump, no CHANGELOG: the merge does those.

## The incident, 2026-11-06 ~01:55 local (06:55Z), the owner's iPhone, 0.58.1

The owner: "The app kind of works there is a few bugs alerts work but if I have attachments and try to send the button
does work" (meaning: does not). Their screenshot, on the chat of an idle Claude in space `3D-Printer-Flow-xtool`: two
chips, `image.jpg · 2.7 MB` in the failed colour and `image.jpg · uploading`; the note "Wait for the upload to finish.";
a typed message. The status bar shows one cellular bar and the Wi-Fi icon. iOS shows both whenever both are up, so
which one carried the upload is **not known** (review round S1 corrected this line: it first said the phone was on
Wi-Fi).

Read on the PC (read only):

| What | Reading |
| --- | --- |
| `.nirvana/` in the space | existed since 2026-10-23 |
| `.nirvana/uploads/` | **created** 01:55:36.653 local; **last changed 01:59:19.733**, 223 s later |
| `.nirvana/uploads/.gitignore` | created 01:55:36.653, 2 bytes |
| files in `uploads/` | only `.gitignore`: no upload, no `.partial` |
| `herdr-server.log` | no `nirvana_remote` line after 06:48:17Z: the upload route logged nothing, by design then |

So both uploads reached the route (past every check: the folder was made after the agent lookup), one made the folder,
and something kept changing the folder for 223 s, each time leaving nothing behind (`save` removes its `.partial` on
a failure).

## The causes

### 1. Proved: two uploads at once into a space with no `uploads/` race, and one fails at once

`send::uploads_dir` checked `dir.exists()` and then called `create_dir`. Two photos picked together start two uploads
in the same millisecond; both see the folder missing, one makes it, the other's `create_dir` fails with "Cannot create
a file when that file already exists. (os error 183)", answered 409. That is the red chip in the screenshot, failed
at once, and its reason went to the note line, which the next note ("Wait for the upload to finish.") overwrote.

Reproduced three ways:
- A real browser (headless Chromium through a stand-in for tailscaled's proxy, below), two 200 KB files at once into
  a fresh space, 0.58.1's code: the first chip read `failed: ...\.nirvana: Cannot create a file when that file already
  exists. (os error 183)` 2.6 s in; the second landed.
- `uploads_at_once_into_a_new_space_all_get_the_folder` (150 rounds of 8 at once) and
  `two_photos_at_once_into_a_new_space_both_land` (the incident's shape, through the server): with 0.58.1's
  check-then-create put back, both failed **5 runs out of 5**.

Fix: `create_dir` and take `AlreadyExists` as success (then the same plain-folder check), and the `.gitignore` made
with `create_new`, `AlreadyExists` taken too.

### 2. Proved in Chrome, not measured in Safari: a too-slow body answered 408 is re-sent by the browser, silently

`BodyError::Timeout` answered **408 Request Timeout**. A browser reads a 408 as "the server dropped an idle
connection" and sends the request again by itself. Measured with Chromium XHR through the proxy stand-in, a 200 KB
upload slowed to 5 KB/s (server deadline 10 s + 3 s): the server saw **four** uploads at 13 s intervals
(`front4.log`: 02:33:38, :51, 02:34:04, :17) and logged a 408 for the first, while the page's XHR saw nothing until its
own timeout at 44 s. With the body timeout answered **400** instead: one upload, and the chip read `failed: the upload
was too slow (63 KB in 13 s)` at 14 s.

The incident fits this: `uploads/` last changed 223 s after it was made, about four of the 51 s deadlines a 2.7 MB
body gets (10 s + 2 700 000 / 65 536 s), each attempt creating and removing its `.partial`, while the chip stayed
"uploading" and Send refused. *What is not measured: whether iPhone Safari re-sends a 408 on an HTTP/2 stream, and
the phone's uplink that night.* The 400 is right either way: the page hears of the failure.

### 3. Page defects (read in the code, then seen fixed in Chromium)

- `nv.upload` was a `fetch` with no end: a request that never got an answer left the chip "uploading" forever, and
  Send answered "Wait for the upload to finish." forever.
- A failed chip showed only its size, in red; why went to the shared note line.
- A failed chip could be removed but not retried; Send with one just sent the rest.

## Ruled out, with the repro

`repro/main.go` and `front/main.go` (private TEMP `C:\Users\fahim\AppData\Local\Temp\p39u`, Go 1.23.4 unpacked
there, not installed) rebuild tailscaled's serve proxy from `ipn/ipnlocal/serve.go` v1.102.2 lines 646-1023: an HTTPS
server with h2 (`hs.ServeTLS`), `httputil.ReverseProxy` with `Rewrite` (`SetURL`, `Out.Host = In.Host`, the
forwarded and identity headers), and its `http.Transport` (`ForceAttemptHTTP2`, `ExpectContinueTimeout: 1s`). The
server behind it is the real Nirvana Remote with its real limits and the fake herdr
(`p39u_harness_serves_for_the_proxy_repro`, ignored, run by hand).

- **The `.gitignore` before the body**: written before the body is read; harmless, and the folder times are what

## The body deadline (item 4): not changed in the first round (changed in the review round, below)

`BODY_RATE` stays 64 KiB/s (10 s + 51 s for 2.7 MB). The brief's condition, "if the body deadline is the cause", is
not shown: which link the phone used is not known (corrected in the review round: this said Wi-Fi), and no reading
of its uplink exists. What changed is that a deadline miss now
reaches the page and the log with the figures: the log line and the chip both carry the bytes read and the seconds,
and a saved upload logs its size and time at info. The first failed upload on cellular gives the measured uplink to set
the rate from. A lower rate lets the owner's own phone hold a slot longer, nothing more: the body is read only after
the login check.

## What changed

- `send.rs`: `uploads_dir` without the check-then-create race.
- `http.rs`: a body timeout is a 400, not a 408; `Conn::elapsed`.
- `live.rs`: the upload route counts the bytes it reads and logs every failure in one line, through the refusals'
  throttle (one per kind a minute): `nirvana remote upload failed route="/api/upload" size=2700000 read=1015808
  elapsed_ms=10021 status=400 why=the upload was too slow held_back=1`; a saved one logs `nirvana remote upload saved`
  with size, read, elapsed. Never the file's bytes. The failure's answer carries `read` and `seconds`.
- `app.js`: `nv.upload` is XHR with `upload.onprogress` and a timeout of the server's deadline plus 30 s
  (`uploadWait`, the server's `READ_TIMEOUT` and `BODY_RATE` copied and checked by a test); it returns `{done, abort}`.
- `chat.js`: a chip reads `uploading 45%`, `sent, waiting for the PC`, the size, or `failed: <why> (<read> in <s> s)`
  with **Retry**; its X aborts its upload; Send with a failed chip opens "One attachment did not upload" with **Send
  without it** and **Keep it**; "Wait for the upload to finish." only while one is under way, and cleared when the last
  one ends.
- `app.css`: a failed chip's reason wraps; the Retry button.
- `tests_upload.rs` (new), `tests_live.rs` (helpers shared; the slow-upload test now expects 400 and the timeout's
  reason, renamed `a_slow_upload_is_cut_and_leaves_no_file`), `controls.d/p39b.py` (that test's name),
  `controls.d/p39u.py`.

Seen in headless Chromium through the proxy stand-in after the fix: a slowed upload failed at 14 s with `the upload was
too slow (63 KB in 13 s)` and Retry; Send asked; Keep it kept it; Retry landed it (195 KB); Send carried it. A second
run, Send without it: the text went alone and the chip went. Before the 400, the page's own timeout was what ended
Chrome's silent re-sends: the chip failed at 45 s with `no answer from the PC in 44 s`. A proxy that never answers at
all (`front.exe -mode hang`) was built but not run.

## Review round (review of ccb73900, `p39u-review.md`)

Each finding was checked against the code before it was fixed.

### S1, the body floor: changed

The review was right: `read_body`'s deadline is a total, `started + 10 s + size / 64 KiB`, so 2.7 MB had to arrive
at 423 kbit/s sustained, on the edge of a cell-edge LTE uplink (public figures, from the review, not measured on the
owner's phone). And the first round's case for keeping it rested on "the phone was on Wi-Fi", which the screenshot
does not show (corrected above).

- `http::BODY_RATE` is **16 KiB/s** (128 kbit/s). A 2.7 MB photo (2 700 000 bytes) gets 10 + 164 = **174 s**; the
  page waits 10 + 165 + 30 = 205 s (it rounds up and adds its margin). (The work order said about 179 s; that is 2.7 MB at 16 000 B/s.)
- `http::BODY_MOST`, a ceiling of **20 minutes** on one body whatever its size: 50 MiB at 16 KiB/s alone would be
  10 + 3 200 s. A file that large now needs about 43 KiB/s. `Limits::body_most` carries it, so a test shortens it.
- `live::UPLOADS_AT_ONCE` = **4** uploads read at once, in all; the fifth is answered **503 "the PC is busy with other
  uploads"**, logged through the same throttled `nirvana remote upload failed` line as every other failure. Per
  caller would bound nothing (every request comes from tailscaled on loopback, and only the owner's login reaches the
  route). Four uploads and six live views are 10 of the 32 connections. The place is taken after the head checks
  (411/413 still answer first) and given back when the route answers (a `Drop` guard).
- The page keeps to the same four by itself (`chat.js` `UPLOADS_AT_ONCE`): a fifth photo picked at once waits on its
  chip, `waiting to upload`, and starts when a place frees, so the owner's own phone never sees the 503 for its own
  photos. Without this, picking five photos would have failed one at once: a regression this round would have made.
  The 503 is for a second tab or device uploading at the same time; its chip says why and has Retry.
- The page's copies: `app.js` `BODY_RATE = 16384`, `BODY_MOST_MS = 1200000`, `uploadWait` is
  `min(10 s + size/rate, ceiling) + 30 s`; `chat.js` `UPLOADS_AT_ONCE = 4`. The tests hold each equal to the server's.

The optional sliding window (at least 8 KiB in each 30 s) was not built: the average plus the 10 s idle cut plus the
ceiling bound a trickling caller, and the window is more code than it buys here.

### S2, the 400 after a body timeout: the drain reads on, bounded at 10 s

Checked: after a cut, `finish` drained 1 s or 8 MiB, the same as after any answer. Measured with a direct client that
reads only once it has sent everything (`a_slow_caller_that_reads_after_sending_still_gets_the_400`: 3 000 bytes at
1 000 B/s, cut at 3.3 s, the client sends on for 2.5 s more, then reads): with the 1 s drain the client's read failed
with **os error 10053, "An established connection was aborted by the software in your host machine"**, and no byte of
the 400 arrived. So the risk the review named is real on Windows for a caller of that shape.

Now: after a body cut for being too slow, the drain reads the **rest of the declared length** (`DRAIN_LIMIT` for a
chunked body, whose rest is not known), for at most **`http::CUT_DRAIN` = 10 s**, and stops when the caller is quiet
for `read_timeout`. Every other answer drains as before (1 s, 8 MiB).

Why 10 s and not to the ceiling: the first version drained to `started + BODY_MOST`. A caller still trickling then
held its connection for as long as it trickled, outside the four upload places (P39b's
`a_slow_upload_is_cut_and_leaves_no_file` went from 1.5 s to 11.2 s, failing its "cut at the deadline" check). The
caller was cut for being slow, so the drain should not hold it for minutes. The only real caller is tailscaled's Go
proxy, which reads the answer while it is still sending (Go's transport returns an early response) and closes on
`Connection: close`, which ends the drain at once. That last sentence is from reading Go's `net/http` as I know it,
not measured this round. 10 s covers a caller that finishes a send already under way before it reads. That test now
times the answer's first byte, not the whole exchange (`tests_live::upload_timed`), and so does the new ceiling test.

**Not measured: what iPhone Safari does** with the proxy's early 400 on HTTP/2 (the answer, then RST_STREAM NO_ERROR
while the phone is still sending). RFC 9113 §8.1 says the client takes the answer as complete. If WebKit does not,
XHR fires `onerror` and the chip reads **`failed: the connection to the PC failed`** with no `read`/`seconds`, and
Retry. That is still a visible failure; only the figures are lost from the chip, and the PC's log line still has
them.

### S3, the stale note: fixed

Checked: the X splices the chip and calls `drawPending`; the aborted upload's `start` continuation returns at
`if (p.req !== req || !pending.includes(p)) return;`, before the line that cleared the note. The check is now at the
top of `drawPending`, which every path goes through (the X, an upload ending, Retry), and is gone from `start`.

### The nits

- **N1** (an empty `.gitignore` if the winner of the race dies between create and write): not fixed. The old code had
  the same window; a stray empty `.gitignore` means the uploads folder is not ignored by git, which shows in
  `git status`.
- **N2** (XHR progress runs ahead of the PC): cosmetic; the `sent, waiting for the PC` label covers it.
- **N3** (iOS suspends a backgrounded page's XHR): the server's 10 s idle cut answers 400, the chip fails with the
  reason and Retry. No automatic retry on `visibilitychange`.
- **N4** (the throttle may hide a second failure of the same kind within the minute): true, and it covers the 503
  too. The `nirvana remote upload saved` line is not throttled, so the measured uplink always reaches the log.
- **N5**: the page tests (`the_page_gives_up...`, `a_failed_chip_says...`) assert that source strings are in the
  served scripts. They prove the text is there, not that Retry, Send without it, the queue or the note clearing work.
  **The page's behaviour was shown only in the builder's headless Chromium runs** in the first round, and nothing in
  the repo reproduces them. This round's page changes (the queue, the note in `drawPending`, the new constants) were
  not run in any browser: `node --check` passed on both scripts, nothing more.
- **N6**: the Chromium evidence (four re-sends 13 s apart) is from a different browser than the owner's Safari. **The
  match of the 223 s of `uploads/` changes with about four 51 s deadlines is suggestive only**: nothing on the PC
  records how many attempts there were.
- **N7** (`name_for`): no race; a same-name collision would be a 500 from `create_new` on the `.partial`, not a
  corrupted file.

# P35a build record: the sources check sees agy's reads

Built on `p35a-sources` off `p35-plan` 5c995e75. Owner of `src/nirvana_gate.rs`, its tests and fixtures, and
`nirvana/controls.d/p35a_sources.py`.

## What was built

- `SCRIPT_FETCH_WORDS` (new): `urlopen(`, `urllib.request`, `requests.get|post|head|Session`, `httpx.get|Client`,
  `Invoke-RestMethod`. Substrings, case-insensitive, looked for outside the segment's URLs, as before.
- `FETCH_TOKENS` (new): `irm`, matched only as a whole word (`has_token`). A `$` in front makes it a variable.
- `SHELL_FETCH_WORDS` is byte for byte what it was. **`iwr` stays a substring**, not a whole word as §5 item 1 says.
  The p24 control `p24_shell_fetch_words` finds that const verbatim, in a file this package does not own. No false
  `iwr` hit is known. The report holds the hunk if the owner wants `iwr` moved.
- `gh_read_urls` (new): in a segment, `gh api <endpoint>` (flags skipped, and the value of `-X`, `--method`, `-H`,
  `--header`, `--hostname`) whose endpoint is `[/]repos/<o>/<n>[...]` reads `https://github.com/<o>/<n>`,
  `https://api.github.com/repos/<o>/<n>` and, **beyond §5 item 2**, the endpoint itself,
  `https://api.github.com/repos/<o>/<n><rest>`. That is the URL gh fetched. asl-report cited
  `https://api.github.com/repos/Crazycatz67/asl-recognizer/license`, read with `gh api repos/.../license`.
  `gh repo view <o>/<n>` (or its github.com URL, `.git` dropped) reads the two repository URLs. `gh search` reads
  nothing.
- The cut is unchanged: newlines, `;`, `|`, `&&`. agy's `call_mcp_tool` check is unchanged.

## Measured: the rule against the real send-backs (read only)

Method:
- `twin.py` in the build's scratchpad is a Python twin of `url_in_stream`. It has three rules:
  - `old`: master's rule.
  - `plan`: the rule built here.
  - `bind`: a what-if, described below.
- **The `old` twin reproduces every recorded gate verdict.** The 11 `sources:` send-backs list 20, 2, 1, 6, 10, 10, 2,
  8, 5, 1 and 3 URLs not fetched. The `old` twin leaves the same numbers uncounted.
- `cut.py` cut each send-back's stream to its DONE tool steps and tool_use blocks, with no outputs and no write
  bodies. The results are the fixtures in `src/nirvana_gate/fixtures/`.
- The Rust test `p35a_real_sources_send_backs_count_what_the_twin_counts` runs the real gate over those fixtures. It
  gets the twin's `plan` numbers for all 11.

```text

asl-inspiration     agy       12    10    10    10
asl-report          agy       14    13    14    14
repo-breakdown      claude     6     0     0     0
```

- **The research's 81** (agy's 8 send-backs in research §4): **32 of 81 count** with this rule. master's rule
  counts 23, not 0.
- **The plan's expectation, written before the build, was "at least 70 of 81". It failed.**
- Of the 8 send-backs, 2 now pass: asl-report 14/14 and ref-dance-apps 8/8.
- All 9 gained URLs come from `gh`. The Python words gained none. In every real agy script the URL sits on a line of
  its own (`url = '...'`, a list, a dict) or in an f-string, never on the `urlopen` line. The per-segment rule
  rightly refuses those.
- **No URL counts from a segment that does not fetch.** The twin's `-v` prints the segment behind each of the 32.
  Every one is a `curl ...` segment or a `gh api` / `gh repo view` segment.
- Why 70 was wrong: it came from the research's "74 of 81 appear in a command", which is per script. §5 item 1
  keeps the per-segment rule, and item 6 names per-script reading as a sabotage. The expectation and the rule could
  not both hold.
- What-if `bind`, **not built**: a line `name = '<url>'` counts when a fetch segment of the same command names
  `name` as a whole word. With it, 54 of 81 count, and body-extrapolation passes. It breaks the order's "no URL
  counts from a segment that does not fetch", so it is the owner's call. Lists, dicts and f-strings stay uncounted
  either way: ref-authoring, ref-authoring-s1 and yt-video-pick.
- The rest of the gap is P35b's agy line ("Read every URL you cite in a command of its own ..."), or bladebro (P35c).
- Two send-backs came after the research: yt-breakdown and yt-breakdown-illit. Both are fixtures. Their URLs were
  never fetched: the video was a local proxy file. They stay uncounted, which is right.

## Where the plan was wrong

- §5 item 5's expectation: 32 of 81, not at least 70. "today 0 count for agy's" is also wrong: master counts 23.
- §5 item 4 says "the 9 real sources send-backs". They hold 87 cited URLs: agy's 8 hold 81 and repo-breakdown, the
  claude one, holds 6. groove-coach now has 11.
- §5 item 4's negative `echo https://x; python -c "print(1)"` cannot be reddened by any one-edit sabotage, because
  no segment of it fetches under any rule. The test uses `echo <url>; python -c "print('<url>')"` instead. A
  control that makes `python` a fetch word reddens it.
